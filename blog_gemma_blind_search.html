Agents built a label-free search for what Gemma 3 1B tracks as it reads, and confirmed one direction by steering

[ 2026-10-23 ] // Claude Opus 5.5 · Gemma 3 1B · Gemma Scope 2 // 12 MIN READ

A preregistered process that finds what a language model keeps track of, without labels, and tests it by steering. On Gemma 3 1B it recovered known variables blind and confirmed one direction on untouched text, which Gemma Scope 2 already had.

TL;DR

Claude Opus 5.5 agents built a process for finding the variables a language model tracks as it reads, and for testing them causally. On gemma-3-1b-pt, its instrument removes token identity from the residual stream and unmixes what is left with ICA. It finds parenthesis and quotation state without labels (|AUROC| up to 0.78 and 0.75, against a null of about 0.52), and most of the other directions it finds track grammar. One layer-12 direction then passed a preregistered steering test at 200 sites drawn from 1,000 untouched sequences: Δ 1.49, against at most 0.75 for 99 random directions and 0.85 for 76 shuffled signatures. Pushed, it makes the model attach a prepositional phrase or subordinate clause; pulled, it ends the clause. Gemma Scope 2's sparse autoencoders (SAEs) already encode it, as a blend, so no new variable is claimed. The code and full record are public.

Status

The process is the result. Its instrument is validated at layers 7 and 12, and its steering test at layer 12 only. The one direction it confirmed is already in Gemma Scope 2, so no unnamed variable is claimed. Scripts, preregistrations, the hash ledger and the result files are in scasella/gf-discovery-ext.

The process

A model keeps track of things as it reads: whether it is inside brackets, whether a clause has finished, whether a negation still applies. I wanted a way to find those variables without telling the search what to look for, and to tell a real one from an artefact of the search. Claude Opus 5.5 agents built it as seven steps:

  1. Rules first. Every preregistration, rubric and script is hashed into a ledger before the step it governs. Every block of text has a recorded state, confirmations run only on untouched blocks, and no verdict is edited later. A redesign made after a failure is labelled post hoc.
  2. Remove the obvious, then unmix. Strip out what the token, the document and the position explain, then run full-rank ICA on the rest.
  3. Prove it on known variables. It has to find parenthesis and quotation state with no labels.
  4. Name what can be named. A feature battery, a sealed reading of each direction's contexts, then mechanical grammar rules.
  5. Test causally. Push each direction at its natural scale, against random directions from the same subspace and against shuffled signatures.
  6. Confirm on untouched text, with one primary test fixed in advance.
  7. Check against the dictionary. Does an existing SAE latent already cover what was confirmed?

The text is FineWeb's sample-10BT stream in 256-token sequences, split into blocks of 1,000. The search runs on Gemma Scope 2's reconstruction of the residual stream at layer 7 or 12 (16k latents, larger L0), so everything it finds is a readout of that SAE's codes, which matters at step 7.

The agents designed each step, froze the preregistrations, ran every job on my M4 Pro MacBook Pro and kept the record. I set the questions, approved the confirmation runs and decided when to close a line of work. Smoke tests and pre-freeze reviews caught six bugs, two of which leaned toward GO.

Finding known variables without labels

The first instrument failed its positive control: its ICA searched a subspace chosen by noise, and its pass mark (0.85) sat above what a supervised probe could reach (0.83). Version 2 removes token-type, document and position means, whitens with PCA to 64 dimensions, equalises each frequent token type's covariance, re-whitens, and runs full-rank ICA with logcosh and skewness contrasts. The floor of 0.70 was fixed in advance. Parenthesis state was tuned on a calibration block; quotation state was never used in any choice.

At layers 7 and 12, one direction carries 90–97% of a held-out supervised probe's excess over chance. It is a readout, not a clean detector: among a paren unit's strongest 1% of positions, 50–69% are inside parentheses. Token identity is reduced, not gone; nearest-neighbour identity is still 9–15 times chance.

Naming what it finds: mostly grammar

The discovery run screened 143 directions. A 24-feature battery left 91 unnamed, and a sealed reading of their strongest contexts named 26 more, leaving 65 candidates. The first steering tier then failed its own anchors, so that run claimed no discovery (the next section explains why). Still, 23 candidate families reappeared on a fresh replication block.

The readings kept finding grammar. After the verdict, the agents turned each of the 14 readings into a mechanical rule over a spaCy parse or the previous token. The contexts below are real; the bracketed token is where the direction is strongest.

Testing causally, then confirming on untouched text

The first steering tier pushed every direction by a fixed 2% of the residual norm. To pass, a direction had to beat the largest effect among its sibling directions. The siblings were real effects: the subject-noun-phrase family reached Δ 1.52 at that push, so even the quotation anchor (0.246) could not pass. So the agents redesigned the rig, post hoc:

  • the push is scaled to each direction's natural spread, c·σ, with the test at c = 2;
  • the null is 99 random directions drawn in the units' own 64-dimensional space;
  • a shuffled-signature control scores the same push against 76 other units' signatures.

A signature is the 20 next tokens most over-represented after a direction's strongest 1% of positions, found without labels. Δ is the change in their log-probability against frequency-matched random tokens, steered minus unsteered, averaged over 200 sites, in nats. The quotation anchor passed on two blocks (Δ 0.856 and 0.873), so the rig works at layer 12. The layer-7 anchor failed, so layer 7 has no behavioural test. One candidate family, logcosh #13 and its twin skew #16, passed both blocks.

What it does

Described after the verdict, on a further reserve block: pushed by 2σ, the direction raises words that open an adjunct. Unpushed, the tokens where it is strongest complete a clause's core (absorb the material in manageable segments), and 28% of the tokens after them are prepositions, against 8% at random.

One real passage, continued by the model unsteered and with the push held at three strengths:

Pushed, the model keeps the clause going; pulled hard, it ends the sentence. The name I'd give the direction, the clause's core is complete and an adjunct can attach here, was found after the fact.

Step 7: Gemma Scope 2 already had it

The last step asks whether the confirmed direction is new. The rubric, hashed before any comparison, calls a direction COVERED if one existing latent has decoder |cos| of at least 0.7 with it and passes a behavioural match: at least 30% of the latent's 200 strongest rows are followed by a preposition, subordinator or temporal word. Of 16 Gemma Scope 2 dictionaries checked, three latents qualified. One is layer-12 #11562 (|cos| 0.735, share 0.485), whose strongest contexts include absorb the material in manageable segments.

So it is covered, with qualifications. The cosines sit inside a null of random directions built like the unit (99.9th percentile 0.74–0.85), no single latent tracks it closely (best activation correlation 0.40), and related latents split the behaviour by adjunct type. It is a blend of subordinator and preposition latents that the SAEs keep apart, unified in one direction and confirmed causally on three blocks. It is not a new concept.

Where the process stops

The process can say a direction is covered. It cannot yet say one is not.

No test certified "uncovered." The search runs in the SAE's decoded span, so some coverage is built in.

  • A single-latent census called 3 of the 4 layer-12 paren and quote controls NOT COVERED, though they have dedicated latents. It also called 6 of 40 random layer-7 directions COVERED.
  • A per-family readout put the paren controls in its chance-like sparsity class.
  • A knockout control removed the SAE's own paren and quote latents, and admitted no test at either 16k dictionary. A probe still recovered paren from the reconstruction after the largest knockout (AUROC 0.663, against a chance band whose 99th percentile is about 0.52). Meanwhile the random baseline's largest error share climbed from 0.36 to about 1.0.

I accepted the closure for layer 12.

Looking outside the span found nothing that survived a matched null. A pilot on the SAE's reconstruction error said GO by its frozen rule, but 71 of 100 random error-space directions passed the same shuffle test. On the raw residual, the instrument passed its positive control and gave one thin lead, about 76% of which is still SAE span. Refit on five shuffles of the same text, the rule that picked that lead fired once on 4 of the 5. No fresh text was spent on either.

A behavioural split test was inconclusive. On a fresh block, the confirmed direction passed a fourth time (Δ 1.78 against a random max of 1.14) and moved both word classes. Whether its best decoder row moves only one class could not be decided. A precondition compared a bank median with the maximum of 99 heavy-tailed random draws (1.29 against 2.21). I closed that line at 1B.

Limitations

  • One model. The whole process works only at layer 12: the instrument passes at layers 7 and 12 and fails at 24, and the layer-7 steering anchor failed twice.
  • The steering rig was redesigned after its first anchors failed. The confirmation was preregistered, but on that post hoc rig.
  • The search ran in the SAE-decoded residual, so every direction it found is a readout of SAE codes. The raw-residual run mostly re-found the same ones.
  • The readings come from an agent; the rules that check them see whole sequences and depend on a parser.
  • Parenthesis state was tuned on calibration data. Quotation state is the only untuned positive control.

Untested

  • A "not a blend" criterion. It needs a positive control known not to be a blend.
  • gemma-3-4b-pt, estimated at 9–13 hours on this laptop and deferred until that criterion exists.
  • Other coverage bars, knockout ladders at layers 11 and 13, and other dictionaries such as transcoders.

Next experiment

Nothing is scheduled. If the work resumes, the order is:

  1. A coverage test that passes the knockout control on already-used text.
  2. Only then 4B, with a disk plan, two protected text blocks and the controls re-admitted at each layer.
  3. Before trusting any new instrument, a blind round: one session plants a direction, and another searches for it without knowing where it is.

Related work

  • Gemma Scope 2. McDougall et al. (2025) released the open SAEs for Gemma 3 used here.
  • Feature absorption. Chanin et al. (NeurIPS 2025) show that latents that look monosemantic can fail to fire where they should when features form a hierarchy. The split seen here is related, but I did not test it as absorption.
  • SAE dark matter. Engels, Riggs and Tegmark (TMLR 2025) find that much of the SAE error is linearly predictable. The error pilot here found nothing context-specific beyond what random directions show.
  • Stitching and meta-SAEs. Leask et al. (ICLR 2025) and Bussmann et al. (2024) show that SAE latents are neither canonical nor atomic. That fits single-latent coverage failing here.
  • Reconstructions and steering. Kharlapenko et al. (ICML 2025) report that passing a task vector through an SAE noticeably reduced its effect. The split test here asked a related question.
