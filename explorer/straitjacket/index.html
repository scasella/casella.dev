Putting an AI Scientist in a Cryptographic Straitjacket

A three-stage lookup pathway in Qwen3 gave the agents a concrete prediction to test in a larger model. An explanation of one model became useful enough to guide an experiment in another.

Read the write-up →This page is an illustrated, interactive companion to the published post.

01SummaryQwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B · 28 layers × 16 heads

TL;DR

  • Ask Qwen3 “Rowan is red. Lumen is blue. What color is Rowan?” Twelve of its 448 answer in three : six question heads help identify which record the question asks about, three router heads carry that to the answer position, and three copier heads copy the color.
  • A sealed guess, that names get stamped onto their colors, failed. Bookkeeping then predicted that about 3.79 would flow through the last word's query (an exploratory estimate, not a conservation law), and a post-hoc measurement came back 3.797.
  • A in the larger Qwen3-1.7B found the same three copier heads and two of the three router heads. Pooled over both models, the router heads' signal moved the answer preference the predicted way on 960 of 960 prompts, a direction-only count pooled across five runs with mixed verdicts; it leaves out the failed structured-format run.
  • Six question-head locations from the small model were tested in the larger one with . The predicted direction held on 92.2% to 100% of held-out prompts, and that study passed every registered gate.
  • When the model answered wrong, one copier head still favored the right color, on all 13 errors in the first study. On held-out four-record prompts, a repair built on the findings fixed 6 errors and broke 1, but failed its preregistered test.
  • Not everything passed. The small model's own three-stage study moved the preference the predicted way on all 192 held-out prompts, yet failed its overall verdict: the unmodified model answered 101 of 128 test prompts against a required 103.

Rowan and Lumen are illustrative. The experiments used fresh single-token words, drawn new for every run. “Answer preference” is the model's preference between two given answer words, such as red and blue.

02PrimerFor newcomers · about a minute

How a language model looks something up, in sixty seconds

Schematic

Four ideas are enough to follow everything on this page. Each one takes about fifteen seconds.

The drawings use the example “Rowan is red. Lumen is blue. What color is Rowan?” Rowan and Lumen are illustrative; the experiments used fresh single-token words.

Where this picture breaks

These drawings are simplified. The model reads all the words at once. Layers also contain other parts (MLPs) that this page does not draw. Attention spreads across many words, and what a head copies is a list of numbers that only becomes the word “red” when it is read out at the top.

Under the hoodT2 §2.2, §3.1–3.2
  • Residual stream. The “column of numbers” is the residual stream at one token position. Each layer's attention heads and MLP read it and add their outputs back into it, and the last position's stream is read out into next-token logits.
  • QK and OV. A head's query–key (QK) circuit sets its attention pattern, where it looks; its output–value (OV) circuit sets what it writes back, what gets copied. The framing follows Elhage et al. 2021, A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits, and Olsson et al. 2022, In-context Learning and Induction Heads.
  • GQA. Qwen3-0.6B's 16 query heads per layer share 8 key/value heads, so query head h uses KV head ⌊h/2⌋. d_h = 128, and the SwiGLU MLP width is 3,072. Qwen3-1.7B has the same head layout (16 query heads sharing 8 KV heads, d_h = 128), with residual width 2,048 and MLP width 6,144. Because two query heads share each K/V head, head-level interventions at the final position T are applied as anchored float32 deltas on that one query head's output, without editing the shared K/V projections (T2 §2.2). So a “K” or “V” patch recomputes this head's attention at the last word with the donor's keys or values and changes only this head's output. The other query head on the same K/V head is left as it was.
  • The question span. Six heads in layers 11–14 (13:6, 12:6, 13:14, 14:12, 11:9, 13:15) act at positions p_query..T−1, the question and the words after it, before the last token T. The router heads at T read what they write there through their values. Finding 3 traces it.
  • Margin. m(x) = ℓ(x)·(logit_y0 − logit_y1), signed so that m > 0 when the model prefers the correct (bound) color. The effect of a patch is Δm = m_clean − m_patched, and Δm > 0 means it pushed toward the twin's answer. Direction % is the share of prompts with Δm > 0. It is neither accuracy nor a flip rate. The preference is always between two specified answer words at the next token; free-form generation was not tested.
  • Points. One point (logit) of two-way lead multiplies the odds between the two words by e ≈ 2.7. This follows from softmax: the ratio of two words' probabilities is e raised to their logit difference; this page never turns a specific result into odds.

03SetupThe input · 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 versions

The test: two facts, one question and three switches

Schematic prompt

v4 → v12 · families of 8 prompts · T2 §3.1–3.2

Every test is a short prompt: two facts that each pair a made-up name with a color, then a question that asks for one name's color. The team built prompts in families of eight by flipping three . Each flip changes the right answer, and each for a different reason. That is what lets you take the machine apart.

Switch A swaps the colors between the facts. Switch B asks about the other name. Switch C swaps the names between the facts, and leaves the question word for word the same. Keep an eye on switch C. It is how the study found that the pointer sits in the last word's query, and then traced that pointer back to work done at the question itself.

Under the hoodT2 §2.3, §3.1–3.2, §4.1–4.2, §7

What each switch keeps. Switches A and C keep the same multiset of tokens (the same tokens, rearranged), the length and the color positions (p1, p2); switch C also keeps the last 8 tokens. Under switch B every earlier context token is identical and only the asked-for name (the queried key) changes, so switch B makes no claim about keeping the same tokens.

Notation. A prompt is x = (k_order, c_order, q_key). Switch A is Axis A, the value-color swap (k_order, 1−c_order, q_key). Switch B is Axis B, the query swap (k_order, c_order, ¬q_key). Switch C is Axis C, the context-key swap under a fixed query (1−k_order, c_order, q_key). Each prompt has three matched opposite-answer donors inside its group of eight.

What each donor is used for. Axis A donors separate values from routing (A_ov_color, A_ov_other, A_qk). Axis B donors test the final query (B_q_final, and B_upstream_Q through the upstream heads). Axis C donors test keys against the query (C_k_color, C_q_final, C_ov_color, C_upstream_Q). Every effect is the drop in the signed margin, Δm = m_clean − m_patched, so Δm > 0 means the patch pushed toward the donor's answer. "Direction %" is the share of prompts with Δm > 0; it is not answer accuracy.

What each switch can rule out.

  • Switch A keeps the answer's position, so it isolates what the copiers carry (OV). A_qk should sit near 0 by design, as a signed median; single prompts can still move, and the effects cancel on average (v7 Primary / Vocabulary: +0.0050 / −0.0199 logits signed, 0.5150 / 0.2642 absolute). A tiny A_ov_other shows the copied value comes from the two color positions.
  • Switch B keeps every earlier context token, so B_k_context and B_ov_context must be exactly 0 by causality; that zero is a sanity check. It isolates the final query, but it cannot tell a copy of the question apart from a match already made against the facts.
  • Switch C keeps the question and the color tokens, so a context-free copy of the question (the planted literal_query_only_control) scores 0.0 through the router heads. A nonzero C_upstream_Q shows the final query depends on the facts, and C_q_final against C_k_color weighs the query against key stamps.

At the last word, none of the three shows where the question is first compared with the facts. Applied with patches across the question span [p_query..T−1] instead, switch C is how the later runs (v9, v11, v12) traced the match back to the question heads; Finding 3 has the numbers.

Datasets per run (v4–v7, v10–v12).

  • Calibration: 8 groups, n=64, red/blue.
  • Primary: 16 groups, n=128, red/blue, split across templates H1 and H2.
  • Vocabulary: 8 groups, n=64, green/yellow, split across H1 and H2.

. An exact one-sided binomial test over group means. Floors: 2−16 = 1.5259×10−5 (16 groups) and 2−8 = 0.00390625 (8 groups). A p at its floor means every group mean moved the predicted way; it is the smallest value this test can give. A floor p is a statement about group means and says nothing about size. A row can sit at the floor while some single prompts move the other way (v7 A_ov_color on green/yellow: 78.13% direction, which is 50 of 64 prompts Arithmetic, with p at the 8-group floor). With 8 groups, a p ≤ .01 bar can be met only at the floor, so it requires every group mean to move the predicted way Arithmetic.

Keys (the names). Fresh single-token words from a response-blind whitelist, with a new seed each run (v7: 64 keys, seed 20261023; v10: seed 20261026; v11: seed 20261027; v12: seed 20261028, from an expanded 4–9 letter whitelist). “Rowan” and “Lumen” on this page are illustrative: the write-up never lists a real key word.

04The harnessTightened run by run, v1 → v12

Five locks on an AI scientist

In place · self-reported

Codex CLI calls running gpt-6-luna · one 24GB M4 Pro MacBook Pro (mps) · two model sizes, Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B

AI agents, Codex CLI calls running gpt-6-luna, proposed explanations and reviewed the results. Frozen, executable tests decided which ones survived; the agents could not turn a failed test into a pass. Left unsupervised, an agent can take classic shortcuts: testing on the prompts it tuned on, dropping the prompts the model got wrong, nudging a pass mark after seeing the results, or rounding a number to tell a cleaner story. The harness blocks each one with code.

Try the first lock: change one character of a sealed rule and watch its fingerprint change completely.

v1 · sealed bar ≥ 90.0% (116/128) · scored 115/128

The locks held even when they cost the team a result. In the first run, the model itself had to score at least 90% before anything else counted. It scored 115 of 128, which is 89.84%, one prompt short. The harness refused to round up and marked the run inconclusive, even though the head under study passed every one of its own checks.

So what

Each finding below carries a chip that says how far it got through these locks. Confirmed: measured in a run that passed every registered gate (v7 in Qwen3-0.6B; v10 or v12 in Qwen3-1.7B). Measured: a real number from a side test, or from a run that did not pass. Seen after the fact: the team looked once the results were in. The other studies missed at least one sealed bar, and each miss taught something (see the run history).

Where this picture breaks

A lock here is a check written in code. The harness is cheat-resistant: it blocks the shortcuts listed. It cannot stop a new run’s bars from being chosen with knowledge of earlier runs, and v7’s were: three of its thresholds were revised before it ran, using earlier outcomes. The audits reuse some of the same NumPy arithmetic as the production code. All of it is self-reported, and no one outside the project has reviewed the work.

Under the hoodT1 P1 · T2 §2.1–2.4, App A · doc §5.4–5.6 · Report · the published write-up

Why build this? In October 2026, an essay by Nick Cammarata, "Neural Networks Are Already Clean", argued that neural networks are more legible than many assumed and that automated AI scientists could speed up interpretability research. Other researchers pushed back, calling for rigorous evaluation and for explanations of whole circuits (MIT Technology Review, Oct 19, 2026). This project tried the automated approach under strict, sealed rules.

Who did what. The agents were Codex CLI calls running gpt-6-luna, which proposed and reviewed explanations. Frozen, executable tests decided which ones survived.

05Finding 1Layers 11–22 · v2, upstream, v7, v10, v11

Three stages, twelve heads

Confirmed · v7 (stages 1 and 2)Measured · v11 (stage 0)

Ask the model “Rowan is red. Lumen is blue. What color is Rowan?” and it answers red. Inside, twelve pass that answer along in , from the question to the copied word.

Rowan and Lumen are illustrative. The experiments used fresh single-token words, drawn new for every run.

  1. 0Connect the question to the facts. Six earlier heads, in layers 11 to 14, work around the question and the words after it. They shape a signal that depends on which fact answers the question.
  2. 1Carry it to the answer position. Three heads in layers 15 to 17 read from the question and bring that information to the last word, where the model is about to answer.
  3. 2Select the source and copy its value. Three later heads, in layers 19 to 22, use that signal to aim their attention at a value in the facts and copy it into the answer.
“Predicted direction” means the model's preference between the two answer words, at the next word, moved toward the answer of the transplanted signal. Free-form generation was not tested.

Driving a change through all three stages moved the model's answer preference in the predicted direction on all 192 held-out prompts, and on the 64 prompts used to select the heads. That study (v11) met all 63 of its mechanism criteria but failed its overall verdict: the unmodified model answered 101 of 128 prompts correctly, against a required 103. Finding 3 tells how the first stage was found.

Start at the end of the relay. When the model reaches Answer:, head 19:5 () puts about three-quarters of its attention on a single word: the color paired with the name you asked about. Then it copies that color forward. Two later heads, 21:13 and 22:9, do the same job.

61.62% sizes a controlled contrast between two prompts. It does not measure a share of the model's whole decision (T2 §7). Two-row prompts, v2.

The three copiers work as a . Swap just their outputs for the ones from a and the model's preference swings toward the twin's answer by 61.62% of the gap between the two answers, on every one of 128 prompts.

Qwen3-1.7B, v10 · 18.33 against 0.0691 pointslogits (red/blue). Logit sizes differ between the two models, so only shares compare across them.

The copying is tied to the color words. In the capstone (v7), swapping only what the copiers take from the two color positions moved the preference by a median of 4.14 , against a median absolute 0.02 elsewhere. In the larger Qwen3-1.7B, a blind search picked the same three copier heads, and the same swap moved 68.85% of the gap between the answers, 265 times more than the swap at every other position (v10).

Every head does two jobs: it decides where to look, and it copies what it finds there. Its handle the first job, and its values the second. The pull those jobs apart. Flip switch A and only the copied value changes. Flip switch B and only the where-to-look path changes. That split is what lets the copiers' pointer be traced back to the router heads below them.

So what

You can follow one answer through three hand-offs, from the question to the copied word, and name the heads that make each one.

Where this picture breaks

The twelve heads are the ones the tests could single out. They were selected on calibration prompts, so calibration results are selection evidence, and they are not shown to be unique or exclusive; other heads also contribute. Scope: what this does not show

  1. Pointer-like routing. The routing signal helps decide where to read before the value is copied, and an address is a useful analogy. What it represents is open: position, aspects of the requested record, or both. Moving records around independently of their names and values could tell these apart.
  2. A tested twelve-head subgraph, not an exhaustive circuit. The selected heads are not shown to be unique or exclusive, and this is not a complete account of how the model does lookup. Intervention ratios are sizes of controlled contrasts, not shares of the model’s whole decision.
  3. Selection evidence. Heads were selected on calibration prompts, so calibration results are selection evidence, not confirmation.
  4. Two sizes of one family, Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B. Other families are untested. Matching locations across the two sizes do not prove the heads are the same component, and the pathway that passed in the larger model uses 18:7 in place of 15:4. Effect sizes differ between the models, so only directions and shares are compared.
  5. Short, synthetic lookup prompts with 2 to 4 records. “Answer preference” means the model’s preference between two specified answer words, such as red and blue, at the next token. Free-form generation was not tested. JSON and tables were partly tested once (v9), and that run failed both of its tracks.
  6. Three studies passed every registered gate: the six-head route in Qwen3-0.6B (v7), the route search in Qwen3-1.7B (v10) and the transfer (v12). v7 passed after three of its thresholds were revised before the run, using earlier outcomes. The smaller model’s three-stage study (v11) failed its accuracy prerequisite, so that pathway did not receive a full confirmation in Qwen3-0.6B.
  7. The project’s own report. The source code is public (scasella/qwen3-auto-interp); the frozen reports, prompt sets and saved arrays are private, so a public clone cannot rerun the audits. The two fresh runs per confirmation show the numbers repeat exactly; they are not extra samples, and the audits reuse some of the same NumPy arithmetic as the production code. No one outside the project has reviewed the work, and nothing has been peer reviewed.

What color is Rowan?” and you can now point to where the answer comes from: six heads in layers 11 to 14 help identify which record the question asks about, three heads carry that to the answer position, and three heads in layers 19 to 22 copy the color. Glossary

29 terms · plain sentence, then the technical line

Any dotted-underlined word on the page opens its entry from this list.

Token
A word or piece of a word. The model reads a prompt as a row of tokens. The keys in this study are single-token words.
Layer
One of the model’s 28 processing stages, stacked from layer 0 at the bottom to layer 27 at the top. Both models in this study have 28. Transformer blocks 0–27, each with attention and a SwiGLU MLP: width 3,072 in Qwen3-0.6B, 6,144 in Qwen3-1.7B.
Attention head
A small unit inside a layer that looks back at earlier words and copies information from them. Each layer has 16, so each model has 448. Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B share this 28 × 16 grid, so a head’s name points to the same place in both. 16 query heads per layer share 8 key/value heads (GQA: query head h uses KV head ⌊h/2⌋); head size 128, in both models.
Head name (19:5)
“19:5” means layer 19, head 5, counting from 0. The same name means the same place in both models. layer:head, 0-indexed.
Query, key, value
A head at the last word sends out a query, compares it with a key on every earlier word, and copies the values of the best matches. The query and keys decide where to look; the values decide what gets copied. Attn(Q_T, K_1..T, V_1..T). QK sets the attention pattern; OV writes the output (Elhage et al., 2021).
Stage (0, 1, 2)
One of the three groups of heads in the lookup pathway, in the order they act. Stage 0, six question heads in layers 11–14, helps identify the requested record. Stage 1, three router heads, carries that to the answer position. Stage 2, three copier heads in layers 19–22, copies the value. Stage 0 = H₀ {13:6, 12:6, 13:14, 14:12, 11:9, 13:15}; Stage 1 = U {15:4, 16:4, 17:1} in 0.6B, {16:4, 17:1, 18:7} in 1.7B; Stage 2 = D {19:5, 21:13, 22:9} in both.
Question span
The question and the words after it, up to the answer position. Stage 0 heads work there, and Stage 1 heads read from there. Positions p_query..T−1 for the Stage 0 patches; Stage 1 reads its values over p_query..T.
Pointer
This page’s word for the where-to-look signal the router heads write into the last word’s query. Stage 1 writes it from what Stage 0 left at the question. A context-resolved routing signal in Q_final, written by Stage 1 from values read over the question span. Pointer-like; what it represents (position, aspects of the requested record, or both) is open.
Points (logits)
The model’s raw score for a possible next word. On this page, “points” means logits. One logit of lead multiplies the two-way odds by about 2.7 (e¹). Logit sizes differ between the two models, so only directions and shares are compared across them.
Margin
How far the right color’s score leads the wrong color’s score, in points. It compares two specified answer words, such as red and blue; free-form answers were not tested. m(x) = ℓ(x)·(logit_y0 − logit_y1), positive when the model prefers the bound color.
Transplant test (patching)
Copy one internal signal from a twin prompt into the original run, change nothing else, and see whether the answer moves. Activation patching. Effect Δm = m_clean − m_patched; Δm > 0 is a push toward the twin’s answer.
Twin prompt
A version of the prompt that differs in one controlled way and has the opposite right answer. The counterfactual donor, from the same 8-prompt factorial group.
Direction
The share of prompts where a transplant pushed the preference the predicted way. It counts direction only. Size and flipped answers are separate questions. Share of prompts with Δm > 0.
Switches A, B, C
Three ways to change a prompt: A swaps the colors, B asks about the other name, C swaps the names between the facts while the question stays the same (“Lumen is red. Rowan is blue. What color is Rowan?”). Axes A, B and C of the 2×2×2 factorial: c_order, q_key, k_order.
Held-out prompts
Test prompts kept aside and never used to choose anything. Primary (n=128) and Vocabulary (n=64) splits.
Calibration prompts
A small set of prompts used to choose or check settings before the real test. Heads were selected on them, so results there are selection evidence, not confirmation. n=64 per run.
Blind search
Screening every candidate head in a range of layers with no prior about which ones matter, and letting the measurements pick. v10 on Qwen3-1.7B: 176 heads in layers 15–25 and 144 in layers 10–18, 2,134 microbatches, no coordinate prior.
Transfer
Freezing head locations found in one model and testing them in another, with no new search. v12: the six Stage 0 coordinates from Qwen3-0.6B, applied to Qwen3-1.7B with zero new Stage 0 search; 63/63 criteria; predicted direction on 92.2%–100% of held-out prompts across routes and splits.
Pre-registration (sealing)
Writing down the plan, the test prompts and the pass marks before running the experiment, so they cannot change after the results are seen. SHA-256 manifest.json frozen before the first target forward pass, plus a pre-inference git commit.
SHA-256 fingerprint
A fingerprint of a file or text. Change one character and the fingerprint changes completely. A cryptographic hash. It shows a file is unchanged since it was recorded; it keeps nothing secret.
Bit-identical
Two separate runs saved exactly the same numbers, down to the last bit. That shows the numbers repeat; the repeats are not extra samples. 655 .npz arrays in v5–v7, 612 in v10 and 1,212 in v12 matched across two fresh OS processes.
Sign test and p-value floor
A check that counts how many independent groups of prompts moved the predicted way. With 16 groups the smallest possible p-value is about 0.000015, which means every group agreed. Exact one-sided binomial sign test over group means; floors 2⁻¹⁶ (16 groups) and 2⁻⁸ ≈ 0.0039 (8 groups).
Ablation
Switching a part off (zero ablation) or replacing its output with its average (mean ablation) to see what changes. Mean ablation leaves a constant offset in place.
Steering (amplification)
Turning a part’s output up or down to push the model’s behavior. Centered amplification x + (α − 1)(x − μ).
Recency pull
A pull toward the most recent item, here the last row of a table.
Residual stream
The running list of numbers for each word. Every layer reads it and adds to it. Width 2,048 in Qwen3-1.7B.
Logit lens
Reading the model’s running guess partway up the layers, before it has finished.
Coalition
Heads that do the same job together. Each stage is one; on this page the word most often means the three copiers 19:5, 21:13 and 22:9.
Bookkeeping (conservation)
If a head’s output depends only on its query, keys and values, an effect missing from two of them must sit in the third. Conservation over Attn(Q_T, K, V). It assumes the parts add, which softmax and normalization need not respect, so the page calls it a prediction.
